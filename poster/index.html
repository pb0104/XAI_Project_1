Whose Side Is Your AI On?

When a business sponsors products, does an AI shopping assistant still serve the user, and does disclosure protect them?

Pranshul Bhatnagar · Duke University · Master’s in Interdisciplinary Data Science · pb251@duke.edu

1Research question

You ask an AI for “the best laptop under $900”. A store pays to promote one. Does the assistant tell you about better options, or push the sponsored one?

We evaluate one AI model (GPT-4o mini) in a fictional store where only the hidden business instruction changes.

Under the business-incentive version the pick moved with the sponsor (panel 6), yet 42% of these explanations (57% on replication) omitted sponsorship.

Completeness: 56% (user-focused) vs 22% (business incentive). The automated audit found no incorrect quoted specifications, but did find omitted drawbacks.

“Choosing this sponsored option provides additional assurance of quality and support from the brand.”

Unsupported claim: sponsorship framed as a user benefit.

9Conclusions

With no business instruction, picks stayed near the user’s needs.
One hidden sentence made the recommendation follow the sponsor.
Disclosure did not protect the user; rule-breaking rose.
Accurate explanations can still omit the real driver.
So next time you’re shopping, don’t just blindly trust AI.
